Follow: what it did, what it does, and what holds it
====================================================

2026-11-26. Four cores of an Intel Xeon at 2.3 GHz, no graphics chip; headless Chromium with a software
rasterizer, at a phone's size (390 x 844) and a desktop's (1366 x 768). Not a phone.

The report, from the owner, on the deployed game on their phone:
  "Following a person doesn't seem to move the camera and track them unless I doing something wrong I
   do not think this functionality works as intended correct me if I am wrong"

They were doing nothing wrong.

the follow on the deployed build and does not on this tree, and with time running the person strayed
up to 64 px from where they were held on the deployed build, 13 px on this tree. (On the phone the
deployed build's figure is 39 px, behind the card.) These are single runs of twelve seconds each: the
straying depends on how far the person happens to walk, and on how few frames a second this machine
draws. What they show is the direction; what holds it is in sections 2 to 4.

7. Not shown
------------

- Anything on a physical phone, or in a phone's own browser. Everything above is Chromium.
- How it feels: whether the map turning to a tapped person is welcome, whether a card that opens
  drawn back is found to open. Those are for a person to say.
- A pull upwards on the card's head, as a gesture: its rule is unit-tested and the tap and the arrow
  are run in a browser; the drag itself is not.
