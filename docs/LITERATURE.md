# Literature and prior work (verified bibliography)

> Verification policy: every entry below was checked on 2026-11-25 against the linked authoritative record. Relevance notes describe how the work informs TABULA RASA's design or caveats; they are our reading, not claims of replication. Entries we could not verify were excluded.

How the entries were checked, and the limits of that check:

- **Records used.** Crossref REST API records for DOIs; arXiv abstract pages; publisher and proceedings pages (NeurIPS, PMLR, ACL Anthology, USENIX, IFAAMAS, JASSS, International Journal of the Commons, NBER, Nature, Cambridge, Princeton, Oxford, Project MUSE, the Nobel Foundation); and bibliographic databases (RePEc, zbMATH Open, Open Library, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, PubMed) for works without a DOI and for abstracts that Crossref does not carry.
- **Omitted fields.** A field that could not be confirmed is left out, and the entry says so (for example the issue number of Holm 1979 and the page range of Pérolat et al. 2017).
- **Depth of reading.** Unless an entry says otherwise, we read the abstract or the publisher's description, not the full text. Where only the bibliographic record could be fetched, the note says so and makes no statement about the work's findings.
- **Blocked pages.** JSTOR, ScienceDirect, science.org, SpringerLink and the MIT Press site refused automated access, and the Basic Books product page redirected to a home page; the affected entries were verified through Crossref or the databases above. The Princeton, Oxford, Cambridge and Project MUSE pages could only be read through a fetch tool that returns a model-written summary. Bibliographic fields taken from those pages were cross-checked against Crossref where a record exists; book descriptions and tables of contents rest on the summaries alone.
- **Status of design statements.** The notes refer to the design recorded on 2026-11-25 in `CHARTER.md`, `AFFORDANCES.md`, `EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md` and `DECISIONS.md`, where most affordances are still marked planned. Where a note suggests something those documents do not contain, it says so. If the design changes, this file must be updated together with `DECISIONS.md`.
- **Scope.** This is a curated working bibliography, not a systematic review. No candidate was excluded for failing verification; corrections made during verification are noted in the entries.

## 1. Agent-based modelling and artificial societies

### Schelling1971
- **Citation:** Thomas C. Schelling (1971). *Dynamic models of segregation*. The Journal of Mathematical Sociology, 1(2), 143–186. https://doi.org/10.1080/0022250X.1971.9989794
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/0022250X.1971.9989794 (abstract read at https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1080/0022250X.1971.9989794)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** The abstract reports "no simple correspondence of individual incentive to collective results" and that individual motives usually cannot be inferred from aggregate patterns. Two of our rules follow from it: macro-measures (inequality, modularity, institution counts) are never used to infer what agents "wanted", and the registered studies are built on deterministic non-LLM policies (reflex and planner), because simple local rules can already produce striking collective patterns against which any Claude arm has to be compared.

### Ray1991
- **Citation:** Thomas S. Ray (1991). *An Approach to the Synthesis of Life*. In C. G. Langton, C. Taylor, J. D. Farmer and S. Rasmussen (Eds.), Artificial Life II (SFI Studies in the Sciences of Complexity, vol. X), pp. 371–408. Addison-Wesley. No DOI; author-hosted scan of the chapter: https://tomray.me/pubs/alife2/Ray1991AnApproachToTheSynthesisOfLife.pdf
- **Verified via:** the chapter scan above (its first-page footer gives the volume, editors, publisher, year and page 371; the scan runs 38 pages); the author's publication list at https://tomray.me/pubs/ ; and the Semantic Scholar record https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/2e140a4435f22a04570e35b1b442a22ae90053bd?fields=title,year,authors,venue,externalIds (title, author, 1991). The author's list gives the series volume as XI; we follow the chapter's own footer (X).
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** The Tierra chapter sets out to synthesise life instead of simulating it, starts from hand-crafted organisms, and asks that the system evolve structures "not designed-in or preconceived by the creator". We borrow that criterion as the test for institutions (the protocol never counts a designer-authored charter as emergent) and the chapter's openness about its hand-crafted starting point as the model for the affordance registry. Our world has no births, replication or selection, so we do not describe institutional change as evolution.

### EpsteinAxtell1996
- **Citation:** Joshua M. Epstein and Robert L. Axtell (1996). *Growing Artificial Societies: Social Science from the Bottom Up*. The MIT Press (copublished with the Brookings Institution). https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/3374.001.0001
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.7551/mitpress/3374.001.0001
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** The publisher's abstract describes Sugarscape as a model in which group formation, cultural transmission, combat and trade "emerge" from agents following a few simple rules. We inherit the bottom-up stance (start from ecology and individual behaviour, build no institutions in) and use the book as the yardstick for the non-LLM arms: any pattern that rule-following baseline agents already produce in our world cannot be credited to LLM cognition. The "few simple rules" are themselves designer-supplied affordances, which is why ours are listed in the affordance registry.

### BedauEtAl2000
- **Citation:** Mark A. Bedau, John S. McCaskill, Norman H. Packard, Steen Rasmussen, Chris Adami, David G. Green, Takashi Ikegami, Kunihiko Kaneko and Thomas S. Ray (2000). *Open Problems in Artificial Life*. Artificial Life, 6(4), 363–376. https://doi.org/10.1162/106454600300103683
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1162/106454600300103683
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** A field-level list of fourteen open problems in artificial life, each described in the abstract as a grand challenge. We cite it for positioning only: TABULA RASA is an engineered sandbox that borrows artificial life's vocabulary of emergence, and we make no claim to have advanced any problem on that list.

### LenskiEtAl2003
- **Citation:** Richard E. Lenski, Charles Ofria, Robert T. Pennock and Christoph Adami (2003). *The evolutionary origin of complex features*. Nature, 423(6936), 139–144. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01568
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/nature01568 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/nature01568 (abstract and figure captions)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Working with digital organisms on the Avida platform, the authors report that complex functions evolved by building on simpler functions that had evolved earlier, a conclusion drawn by following genotypes along the line of descent. The lesson we take is methodological: a claim about how a complex institution arose needs the complete recorded history of its precursors, not an end-state snapshot. This motivates the append-only event ledger with causal parents and the institution timelines built on it.

## 2. Cooperation theory

### Trivers1971
- **Citation:** Robert L. Trivers (1971). *The Evolution of Reciprocal Altruism*. The Quarterly Review of Biology, 46(1), 35–57. https://doi.org/10.1086/406755
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1086/406755 (abstract read at https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1086/406755)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Presents a model for the selection of reciprocally altruistic behaviour and against the non-reciprocating "cheater", and argues that trust, suspicion, gratitude and moralistic aggression regulate such a system. This is the rationale for the per-acquaintance bookkeeping that every baseline agent keeps (gifts given and received, promises kept and broken) and for logging a broken promise as an event. The argument is about natural selection; our agents do not reproduce or evolve, so we take from it mechanisms to represent, not a dynamic the model reproduces.

### AxelrodHamilton1981
- **Citation:** Robert Axelrod and William D. Hamilton (1981). *The Evolution of Cooperation*. Science, 211(4489), 1390–1396. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7466396
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/science.7466396
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** From a model and a computer tournament, the abstract reports that cooperation based on reciprocity can get started in an asocial world, thrive among other strategies and resist invasion once established. It is the theoretical basis for the conditional cooperation that the baseline agents' trust bookkeeping enables, and for the protocol's reciprocity measure (transfer dyads reciprocated within a time window). The model assumes that pairwise interactions occur on a probabilistic basis, so encounter rates are a covariate worth reporting next to cooperation measures; the protocol does not yet register one.

### Axelrod1984
- **Citation:** Robert Axelrod (1984). *The Evolution of Cooperation*. New York: Basic Books. x, 241 pp. ISBN 0465021220. Catalogue record: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL3186143M
- **Verified via:** https://openlibrary.org/books/OL3186143M.json (edition record: Basic Books, New York, 1984, "x, 241 p.") and https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2620286W.json (description); cross-checked against the Crossref record of a contemporary review that gives "New York: Basic Books, 1984. Pp. x + 241", https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/1956233
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** The catalogue description frames the book as asking how cooperation can emerge among self-seeking egoists "when there is no central authority to police their actions". That is the starting condition TABULA RASA imposes (no built-in government or law), which makes the book our reference for interpreting runs in which no institution is ever ratified. We verified the bibliographic record and description only and did not consult the text.

### Nowak2006
- **Citation:** Martin A. Nowak (2006). *Five Rules for the Evolution of Cooperation*. Science, 314(5805), 1560–1563. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1133755
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/science.1133755
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Names five mechanisms for the evolution of cooperation: kin selection, direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity and group selection. We use the list to read the affordance registry. The world affords direct reciprocity (memory of acquaintances), indirect reciprocity (hearsay within hearing range, weighted by trust in its source) and network reciprocity (spatial locality), but not kin or group selection, because the engine has no kinship, births or inheritance (whether to add them as a labelled optional treatment is open question U1 in the charter).

## 3. Institutions and the commons

### Hardin1968
- **Citation:** Garrett Hardin (1968). *The Tragedy of the Commons*. Science, 162(3859), 1243–1248. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.162.3859.1243
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/science.162.3859.1243 (bibliographic record and subtitle only)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** The article that named the problem; a later entry (Pérolat et al. 2017) summarises the "tragedy of the commons" as the prediction that self-interested appropriators fail to reach socially positive outcomes. Our renewable stocks are built so that this outcome is reachable (bushes and fishing waters recover very slowly once nearly emptied), because a commons that cannot fail would make every sustainability result meaningless. Collapse is the reference outcome that reciprocity, communication or institutions have to beat, not a foregone conclusion.

### Ostrom1990
- **Citation:** Elinor Ostrom (1990). *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action*. Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1017/CBO9780511807763 ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/governing-the-commons/7AB7AE11BADA84409C34815CC288CD79 (description and contents)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Per the publisher's description, the book critiques the state-control and privatisation models often recommended for shared natural resources and uses case studies to show when such resources have been governed successfully, sometimes over long periods by self-organised user groups, and when they have not. It fixes our outcome space: collapse and sustained self-governance must both be possible, and neither is scripted. Its "design principles" (as Ostrom 2010 summarises them in an updated list: boundaries, congruence with local conditions, collective-choice arrangements, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution, recognition of rights, nested enterprises) may be used only as a post-hoc descriptive vocabulary for charters that agents ratify. They are never given as examples in agent prompts, and designer-authored charters (the scripted control, the grammar-search skeletons) are disclosed as designer priors.

### North1990
- **Citation:** Douglass C. North (1990). *Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance*. Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511808678
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1017/CBO9780511808678 (publisher's abstract)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Defines institutions as the constraints humans devise to structure interaction under uncertainty, separates them from the organisations created to exploit the opportunities an institutional framework offers, and treats institutional development as path-dependent. We keep the distinction in our vocabulary: a charter (the rules) is not the group of agents bound by it, and the protocol's persistence criterion is defined on a charter that is still active with members. Path dependence is why every intervention forks the timeline, so that a treated history can be compared with its untouched twin.

### OstromWalkerGardner1992
- **Citation:** Elinor Ostrom, James Walker and Roy Gardner (1992). *Covenants with and without a Sword: Self-Governance Is Possible*. American Political Science Review, 86(2), 404–417. https://doi.org/10.2307/1964229
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/1964229
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Characterises common-pool resources (subtractable yield, non-trivial exclusion) and presents laboratory experiments built around three arrangements: communication alone ("covenants"), sanctioning alone ("swords"), and communication combined with sanctioning. It is the precedent for treating communication (our hearing radius) as an experimental factor alongside institutional affordances. Because sanctions exist in TABULA RASA only inside a charter (bond forfeiture, suspension of entitlements, expulsion), the nearest registered analogue of the paper's design is hearing radius crossed with the institution-affordance factor, and a treatment that removes only sanction clauses would be a protocol addition. We cite the design only, since the abstract we read does not state the results.

### Greif1993
- **Citation:** Avner Greif (1993). *Contract Enforceability and Economic Institutions in Early Trade: the Maghribi Traders' Coalition*. American Economic Review, 83(3), 525–548. No DOI; stable record: https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v83y1993i3p525-48.html
- **Verified via:** the RePEc record above (with abstract); cross-checked against the OpenAlex record https://api.openalex.org/works/W1543801066
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** A historical and game-theoretic study of a merchants' coalition in which, per the abstract, expectations, implicit contractual relations and a specific information-transmission mechanism supported a reputation mechanism that let traders employ distant agents despite the commitment problem. It is the background for two affordances: a charter may expel a member as a consequence of breach, and escrowed pacts, which make exchange possible without reputation, can be switched off to study trust-based exchange. It is also one reason communication range is a registered factor (H3): arrangements that rest on shared information need that information to travel.

### CrawfordOstrom1995
- **Citation:** Sue E. S. Crawford and Elinor Ostrom (1995). *A Grammar of Institutions*. American Political Science Review, 89(3), 582–600. https://doi.org/10.2307/2082975
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/2082975
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Introduces the institutional grammar: a syntax that identifies the components of institutional statements and sorts statements into rules, norms and shared strategies. The component names (Attributes, Deontic, aIm, Condition, Or else: ADICO) are taken from the secondary sources we fetched (Ghorbani et al. 2013; Ghorbani and Bravo 2016); the APSR abstract itself names only the three statement types. Decision D-006 makes the regulative part of a charter a set of typed, executable statements in this form: whom the statement binds, a deontic (may, must, must not), an aim (an engine action), conditions (bounded formulas over public facts) and an optional bounded "or else". The three statement types also suggest how to describe what emerges (a ratified statement with a sanction, one without, a behavioural regularity with no ratified statement); any such description is a narrative label kept apart from the verified emergence ladder.

### FehrGachter2000
- **Citation:** Ernst Fehr and Simon Gächter (2000). *Cooperation and Punishment in Public Goods Experiments*. American Economic Review, 90(4), 980–994. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.90.4.980
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1257/aer.90.4.980 (abstract read at https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.90.4.980)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Reports that free riders are heavily punished even when punishing is costly and brings the punisher no material benefit, and that the opportunity for costly punishment causes a large increase in cooperation. It is the human-subject evidence for letting charters carry bounded sanctions at all. Our sanctions differ in a way that matters: they are bounded consequences executed by the engine under a ratified rule (bond forfeiture, suspension, expulsion), not a punishment that one agent pays to inflict on another, so the costly peer punishment studied here is not reproduced, and that limit should be read alongside any sanction result.

### Ostrom2005
- **Citation:** Elinor Ostrom (2005). *Understanding Institutional Diversity*. Princeton University Press. 376 pp. ISBN 9780691122380. https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691122380/understanding-institutional-diversity (e-book DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400831739)
- **Verified via:** the publisher page above (published September 2005, copyright 2006); https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1515/9781400831739 ; contents at https://muse.jhu.edu/book/64822 ; cross-checked against the Crossref record of a published review that gives "Princeton University Press, 2005, xv + 376 pp", https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/s11127-007-9157-x
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Sets out the Institutional Analysis and Development (IAD) framework and, in Part II ("Focusing on Rules"), a chapter on the grammar of institutions and a chapter on classifying rules. The seven-way classification we use (position, boundary, choice, aggregation, information, payoff and scope rules) is attributed to this book by Ostrom (2010), where we read the list. Decision D-006 organises the grammar by it: roles (position), membership terms and exit (boundary), decision procedures (aggregation), controlled stores (scope), regulative ADICO statements, which play the part of choice rules in our reading, and automatic transfers, bonds and sanctions (payoff). D-006 names no construct for information rules, and rule expressions range over public facts only; we flag this as a gap in our coverage of the typology.

### GurerkEtAl2006
- **Citation:** Özgür Gürerk, Bernd Irlenbusch and Bettina Rockenbach (2006). *The Competitive Advantage of Sanctioning Institutions*. Science, 312(5770), 108–111. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1123633
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/science.1123633
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** An experiment in which a sanctioning institution competes with a sanction-free one; the abstract reports that, despite initial aversion, the whole population migrated to the sanctioning institution and cooperated strongly while the sanction-free one was depopulated. It is the reference for three features of the institution substrate: institutions may coexist and compete (phase prompt 09), any member can leave at any time, and membership is recorded exactly, so migration between institutions can be measured. Whether our agents move toward charters with sanctions is exploratory in the current protocol, not an expectation built into the engine.

### JanssenEtAl2010
- **Citation:** Marco A. Janssen, Robert Holahan, Allen Lee and Elinor Ostrom (2010). *Lab Experiments for the Study of Social-Ecological Systems*. Science, 328(5978), 613–617. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1183532
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/science.1183532 ; abstract read in the PubMed record (PMID 20431012) at https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=20431012&rettype=abstract&retmode=text
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Introduces an experimental environment with spatial and temporal resource dynamics and reports that costly punishment was used but had no gross positive effect on harvesting unless combined with communication. It is the closest human-subject analogue of our spatial, regrowing resources, and its result is a reason to manipulate hearing radius together with the institution affordance (H3) instead of studying sanctions alone; an explicit sanction-by-communication interaction is not registered and would be exploratory. The abstract closes by urging careful generalisation from the laboratory to policy; that caution applies with more force to us, since our "participants" are programs.

### Ostrom2010
- **Citation:** Elinor Ostrom (2010). *Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems*. American Economic Review, 100(3), 641–672. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641 (prize-lecture text of the same title, delivered 8 December 2009: https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/ostrom_lecture.pdf)
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1257/aer.100.3.641 for the journal record; content read in the Nobel Foundation's lecture text linked above. We did not compare the two texts.
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** The lecture lists seven types of rules (boundary, position, choice, information, scope, aggregation, payoff) as external variables acting on the working parts of an action situation, and notes that many variants of each type were found in field cases, for example 27 boundary rules. That diversity is the argument for a generic grammar over a fixed menu of institutions, and the list is a checklist for grammar coverage. The lecture also stresses that the "design principles" were regularities identified by the analyst, not principles the resource users had in mind; we keep the same distinction between our description of an institution and what agents proposed.

### HadfieldWeingast2012
- **Citation:** Gillian K. Hadfield and Barry R. Weingast (2012). *What Is Law? A Coordination Model of the Characteristics of Legal Order*. Journal of Legal Analysis, 4(2), 471–514. https://doi.org/10.1093/jla/las008
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1093/jla/las008 (which gives the authors' initials only); full names and abstract from https://academic.oup.com/jla/article/4/2/471/914703 and https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1093/jla/las008
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Builds a rational-choice model of legal order under exclusively decentralised enforcement (collective punishment) and argues that equilibrium requires an institution supplying a common logic for classifying behaviour as wrongful or not. In TABULA RASA the engine supplies much of both halves: it is a "perfect notary" that records consent, membership, deposits, obligations and votes exactly, and it executes the bounded consequences a charter allows (bond forfeiture, suspension, expulsion). That is the opposite of the paper's setting, in which enforcement has to be coordinated among the governed, so we disclose the notary as a strong designer affordance and do not read engine-enforced compliance as evidence about decentralised legal order.

### FrantzSiddiki2021
- **Citation:** Christopher K. Frantz and Saba Siddiki (2021). *Institutional Grammar 2.0: A specification for encoding and analyzing institutional design*. Public Administration, 99(2), 222–247. https://doi.org/10.1111/padm.12719
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/padm.12719
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Presents Institutional Grammar 2.0, a revised specification for encoding and analysing institutional design that builds on existing Institutional Grammar research. It is the reference we would use for any coding manual that classifies ratified charters, and a check that our canonical AST serialisation does not conflate components the specification keeps apart. Network structure is exploratory in the protocol, and with about 50 nodes we treat modularity as a descriptive statistic, not as proof of factions.

### BlondelEtAl2008
- **Citation:** Vincent D Blondel, Jean-Loup Guillaume, Renaud Lambiotte and Etienne Lefebvre (2008). *Fast unfolding of communities in large networks*. Journal of Statistical Mechanics: Theory and Experiment, 2008(10), P10008. https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/2008/10/P10008
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1088/1742-5468/2008/10/P10008
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** The community-detection method commonly called the Louvain method; its abstract describes it as a heuristic based on modularity optimisation. The protocol computes modularity over communities found by a greedy agglomerative procedure, and this paper is our reference for modularity-optimising heuristics of that kind and for the caution that the partition found is a heuristic result. Whichever optimiser is implemented must fix iteration order and tie-breaking so that the measure is deterministic under replay.

### LeeEtAl2015
- **Citation:** Ju-Sung Lee, Tatiana Filatova, Arika Ligmann-Zielinska, Behrooz Hassani-Mahmooei, Forrest Stonedahl, Iris Lorscheid, Alexey Voinov, J. Gary Polhill, Zhanli Sun and Dawn C. Parker (2015). *The Complexities of Agent-Based Modeling Output Analysis*. Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation, 18(4), 4. https://doi.org/10.18564/jasss.2897
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.18564/jasss.2897 ; https://www.jasss.org/18/4/4.html (abstract and open full text)
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** A review of how to analyse and report agent-based model output; the abstract highlights variance stability in connection with choosing the number of runs and with hypothesis testing, sensitivity analysis, and communicating results. It backs the protocol's "precision before power" rule (the confirmatory seed count is chosen from a pilot estimate of variance to reach a target interval width), the choice to lead with effect sizes and intervals when the number of runs can be made arbitrarily large, and the case for reporting sensitivity to key parameters next to headline results.

### NosekEtAl2018
- **Citation:** Brian A. Nosek, Charles R. Ebersole, Alexander C. DeHaven and David T. Mellor (2018). *The preregistration revolution*. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 115(11), 2600–2606. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114
- **Verified via:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1073/pnas.1708274114
- **Relevance to TABULA RASA:** Argues that preregistration (fixing the research questions and analysis plan before observing outcomes) separates prediction from postdiction and so protects the credibility of findings. In a simulation the temptation is stronger, because outcomes can be regenerated at will. The experiment protocol is therefore written as a pre-registration: hypotheses, the operational definition of emergence, outcome measures, holdout seeds and the analysis plan are fixed before any confirmatory batch, deviations are logged, and exploratory runs are stored and displayed separately.

## How this literature shaped the design

Each point below refers to the design as recorded in `CHARTER.md`, `AFFORDANCES.md`, `EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md` and `DECISIONS.md` on 2026-11-25. Most of it is still to be implemented and tested.

- **Model description follows ODD.** The experiment protocol commits the model description to ODD (GrimmEtAl2006, GrimmEtAl2010, GrimmEtAl2020), and agent decision-making should get its own section for each decision policy, after ODD+D (MullerEtAl2013).
- **Bottom-up start, with non-LLM arms as the reference.** Simple local rules already generate collective patterns (Schelling1971, EpsteinAxtell1996), and a non-LLM model already produces institutional emergence in a commons (GhorbaniBravo2016). The registered studies therefore run on deterministic policies, with a non-LLM institutional-search arm and a scripted positive control, and a pattern that also appears without Claude is not attributed to Claude.
- **Resources are common-pool resources that can really fail.** Renewable stocks are subtractable, hard to exclude others from, spatial and regrowing, and recover very slowly once nearly emptied (Hardin1968, Ostrom1990, OstromWalkerGardner1992, JanssenEtAl2010, PerolatEtAl2017). Neither collapse nor sustained use is scripted.
- **The institution grammar is ADICO statements organised by the IAD rule types (decision D-006).** The regulative part of a charter restricts the Crawford–Ostrom syntax to typed, executable components (CrawfordOstrom1995); the rule typology organises the constitutive parts and is the coverage checklist (Ostrom2005, Ostrom2010); IG 2.0 is the reference for any coding manual (FrantzSiddiki2021); a charter and the group bound by it are kept distinct (North1990).
- **Rule change is a process inside the world, and obligations can be breached.** The charter lifecycle from proposal to dissolution, and the separation between an obligation and an agent's decision to honour it, follow earlier institutional agent-based models (SmajglEtAl2008, GhorbaniEtAl2013).
- **Communication range is a registered factor, and sanctions live inside charters.** Hearing radius is manipulated (H3) because the commons experiments tie what sanctioning achieves to communication (OstromWalkerGardner1992, JanssenEtAl2010, PiattiEtAl2024). Sanctions are limited to bond forfeiture, suspension and expulsion under a ratified charter, which is not the costly peer punishment studied by FehrGachter2000.
- **Institutions may compete, members may always leave, and engine enforcement is disclosed.** Membership is recorded exactly, so migration between institutions can be measured (GurerkEtAl2006); escrow can be switched off to study exchange that rests on trust and reputation (Greif1993); the engine's role as notary and enforcer is listed as a designer affordance because it removes the coordination problem of decentralised enforcement (HadfieldWeingast2012).
- **A deterministic engine adjudicates, and each agent sees only its own information.** Model outputs are proposals checked against a fixed action vocabulary, in contrast to a Game Master that translates free-text actions into effects (VezhnevetsEtAl2023); the memory architecture follows the generative-agents pattern without its believability-based evaluation (ParkEtAl2023); contexts are per agent and non-omniscient (ZhouEtAl2024).
- **Cooperation is measured from engine events, with the available mechanisms listed.** The affordance registry shows which cooperation mechanisms the world affords (Nowak2006, Trivers1971, AxelrodHamilton1981). Outcome measures are computed from logged events, never from self-reports; transfer counts are read together with commons health (LeiboEtAl2017) and holdings inequality (PerolatEtAl2017); the emergence ladder separates adoption, use and persistence from material effect (KosterEtAl2022); modularity uses the standard definition with a deterministic optimiser (Newman2006, BlondelEtAl2008).
- **Every claim about an institution's origin must be traceable in the event ledger.** The "not designed-in" criterion and lineage tracing come from artificial life (Ray1991, LenskiEtAl2003); the affordance registry records what was designed in, and the origin of every institution is recorded.
- **The lab protocol is pre-registered and reports uncertainty.** Hypotheses, the emergence ladder, holdout seeds and the analysis plan are fixed before confirmatory runs (NosekEtAl2018); the registered family is Holm-corrected (Holm1979); paired differences carry bootstrap intervals (Efron1979); seed counts are set by precision from a pilot (LeeEtAl2015); manifests record model identifier, prompt version and seed, and reproducibility rests on recorded replay (VallinderHughes2024, BisbeeEtAl2024). Matched seeds (common random numbers across conditions) are a standard paired design that the protocol adopts without a source from this bibliography.
- **Caveats are part of the product.** Pretraining contamination and model priors (CarliniEtAl2021, SainzEtAl2023, AherEtAl2023, ArgyleEtAl2023), designer-supplied affordances (DaiEtAl2024 as an illustration), the unresolved validation problem (LarooijTornberg2025, GaoEtAl2024) and the limits of LLMs as stand-ins for people (WangEtAl2025) are documented in the affordance registry and the experiment protocol, and the registry is mirrored in the app.

## What we do NOT claim

- **Not human societal emergence.** Anything that emerges in TABULA RASA emerges inside a designed model with designer-chosen physics, actions and rule grammar. We do not claim that it reproduces, predicts or explains how institutions arose in human societies.
- **Not blank-slate minds.** Only the world starts blank. An LLM-driven agent carries the cultural priors of its training data, and we do not claim that any institution it proposes was independently invented.
- **Not a neutral grammar.** The ADICO/IAD typology, the action vocabulary, the engine's perfect record of consent, lockable stores, escrow and forfeiture are theoretical and technical affordances that shape what can be expressed. What the grammar cannot express cannot become an executable institution, whatever agents say to one another.
- **Not validated against human data.** Nothing in TABULA RASA has been calibrated to, or validated against, human behavioural data. Similarity between a run and a pattern described in the literature is not validation.
- **Not general across models, prompts or parameters.** Results hold for the model identifiers, prompt versions, world versions, parameter ranges and seeds recorded in the run manifests, and no further.
- **Not a replication of any cited study.** The human-subject and simulation findings cited above are design inputs. We do not claim that they hold for our agents or that our runs replicate them.
- **Not free of selection bias.** Runs chosen for the observer mode, screenshots and replays are selected because they are interesting. Only the pre-registered confirmatory batches, reported in full with failed and missing runs, support statistical claims.
- **Not a systematic review.** This bibliography is a curated working list that favours well-known English-language work. Relevance notes rest on abstracts or publisher descriptions unless an entry says otherwise.
- **Not scientific novelty, and nothing about minds.** We claim no new scientific discovery and nothing about consciousness, understanding or experience in the agents.
